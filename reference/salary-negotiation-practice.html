Salary negotiation practice

Use this with lesson 0642. These scripts are editable practice wording, not promises about an employer's response. Research the actual role, level, location and employment arrangement before choosing a range. No market salary or legal rule is assumed here.

Prepare before the call

Write down your preferred package, your private minimum acceptable package and your best realistic alternative if no agreement is reached. The alternative might be staying in your current role or continuing the search; it does not need to be another offer. Do not invent competing offers.

Ask for a breakdown of fixed cash, target bonus, equity, one-time payments and benefits. For an Indian CTC quote, ask which components are recurring cash and which are employer contributions, variable amounts or benefits. CTC is not monthly take-home pay. For a contractor role, compare leave, insurance, expenses and payment terms separately. Get qualified local advice for tax or contract questions.

Compare offers on the same basis

Fictional example in lakhs of the same currency: A offers 30 fixed, 3 target bonus and 4 signing payment. B offers 33 fixed and 2 target bonus. A's first-year cash is 37 if the bonus pays in full, then 33 in later years under unchanged terms. B is 35 with its full bonus. Guaranteed recurring fixed cash is 30 versus 33. Neither target bonus is automatically guaranteed. Exclude equity from cash totals and compare its uncertainty separately.

A signing payment can solve a first-year gap but does not permanently raise base pay. Check repayment conditions. Do not count a full multi-year equity grant as annual cash or assume private equity can be sold. Ask for the actual plan documents and written terms.

Questions and sample responses

1. “What salary are you expecting?”

“Could you share the budgeted range and level for this role? I would like to understand the fixed and variable components before giving a useful range.”

If a number is required, use a researched range you would genuinely consider: “For the scope we discussed, I am targeting [range] in [currency] annual fixed pay, depending on the full package.” Specify fixed versus total compensation. Do not quote an arbitrary percentage increase.

2. “What is your current compensation?”

“I would prefer to focus on this role's responsibilities and budget. My target for this opportunity is [range and basis].” If you choose to disclose, describe components accurately. Do not alter payslips or misstate variable pay as guaranteed.

Follow-up: if the employer insists, ask why the information is needed and what alternatives they accept. Local rules vary; do not assume a universal disclosure obligation or prohibition.

3. “This is our offer. What do you think?”

“Thank you. I am interested in the role. Could you send the full breakdown and decision deadline in writing? I would like to review the package and the outstanding questions.”

Then identify one or two priorities. Avoid an immediate acceptance if you still intend to negotiate. There is no guaranteed risk-free negotiation strategy.

4. How do you make a counteroffer?

“Based on the [specific scope] and my experience with [relevant responsibility], could we move the fixed component to [amount]? I am particularly interested in the work on [specific problem].”

Use supported evidence about scope and comparable roles. Your personal expenses alone do not establish the role's compensation level. Pause after the request and listen.

5. “Base pay is at the top of the band.”

“Is there flexibility in the signing payment, level, leave or start date? Which parts of the package can you review?”

Do not assume every component is negotiable. A future review is not a guaranteed increase. Ask for its timing and criteria, then evaluate the offer as it stands.

6. “Do you have another offer?”

Answer truthfully. “I am speaking with other teams, but I do not have another offer” is sufficient if accurate. If an offer exists, explain only details you are entitled to share. Never manufacture a deadline or a competing amount.

Follow-up: a recruiter asks for confidential documents. Ask whether a non-confidential summary is sufficient and respect any commitments you made.

7. “You must decide today.”

“I want to make a considered decision. Could we extend the deadline to [specific date] so I can review the written terms?”

They may decline. Decide using your priorities and alternative; do not accept solely because pressure makes comparison uncomfortable. Clarify when the offer actually expires.

8. How do you close the negotiation?

Summarize the agreed components and remaining conditions in writing. Ask for a revised offer if terms changed. Confirm the role, level, start date and payment conditions before accepting through the requested process.

If declining: “Thank you for the discussions. After reviewing the scope and package, I have decided not to proceed.” A clear, respectful decision is enough.

Rehearse with a partner

Use the fictional offers above. One person is the recruiter with a fixed base-pay limit. The other must clarify the package, make one justified request and respond to a refusal without inventing leverage. Switch roles. Check arithmetic, clarity, listening and whether the final terms are explicit.

Repeat the conversation tomorrow without memorizing a script. Ask your teacher to review your rationale before inserting real numbers. General preparation sources: Harvard offer evaluation guidance and Stanford negotiation guidance. These do not establish current pay for your target role.
