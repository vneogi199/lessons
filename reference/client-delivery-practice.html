Before shipping a change, use the AI coding and security-review exercise.

The worked delivery pack supplies discovery questions, a five-step process, HLD/LLD, API/data contracts, a failure sequence, an ADR, SOW boundaries, ROI arithmetic, UAT and an operations/training handoff. All case figures are synthetic.

Measure the queue before tuning the model

Imagine a form that takes five minutes to approve but waits three hours in an inbox. Making a model five seconds faster barely changes the wait. In the worked case, approval waiting is 180 of 241 elapsed minutes. The first experiment is reviewer coverage and scheduling, while preserving the approval requirement.

Handling time measures time spent doing work. Waiting time measures time in a queue. The distinction also changes ROI: reducing elapsed waiting does not automatically save paid labor.

Make targets testable

The pack's illustrative machine p95 target is eight seconds under a stated load and input cap. Human waiting and asynchronous ticket creation are separate. The target includes a measurement window, workload assumptions and breach response. It is not a measured result or customer promise.

The UAT table leaves actual results as Not run. Each future run needs a build digest, fixture ID, configuration versions and redacted evidence. Critical permission failures block acceptance even when average answer quality improves.

Continue with the cross-account role and budget exercises and GitHub Secrets administration lab. They supply positive/negative checks and cleanup ownership without authorizing external changes.

Interview: why include a failed ticket request in the architecture?

The remote service may create a ticket before its response is lost. The system must preserve an uncertain state and reconcile it. A happy-path diagram hides that decision.

Practice: who decides whether a residual risk is acceptable?

The named business/security risk owner. An engineer documents evidence and tradeoffs; they do not silently waive the acceptance condition.
