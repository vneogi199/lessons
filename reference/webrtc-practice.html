Connect a call when the browsers cannot reach each other directly

Read the connection example first. Then trace the offer/answer sequence and complete the failure table. Browser, camera and TURN-server tests are future authorized exercises, not executed results.

WebSocket and WebRTC solve different problems

A WebSocket maintains a client/server message channel. It suits notifications, application events and signaling. WebRTC supplies peer-connection APIs for real-time media and data channels, including network traversal and media transport behaviour. A call can use WebSocket signaling and WebRTC media at the same time.

Imagine calling a friend. Signaling is how you agree whom to call and what kind of conversation to have. ICE tests possible roads between you. STUN helps discover a public-facing address. TURN provides a relay road when direct communication fails. These are different jobs.

STUN is not a media relay, and a STUN response does not guarantee that another peer can reach you. TURN relays traffic and adds bandwidth cost. WebRTC does not guarantee a direct peer-to-peer path. See WebRTC connectivity.

Trace one connection

Browser A ── authenticated signaling service ── Browser B
   offer/answer and ICE candidates travel through signaling

Browser A ══ selected direct path OR TURN relay ══ Browser B
   media/data use the negotiated connection
  1. Authenticate each participant and authorize the room on the server. A guessed room ID must not grant entry.
  2. Create RTCPeerConnection with approved ICE server configuration. Get camera/microphone permission only after a user action if media is needed.
  3. Add local tracks or create a data channel before negotiating. A creates an offer and sets its local description; send that description through signaling.
  4. B sets the remote offer, creates and sets its answer, then sends it back. A sets the remote answer.
  5. Exchange ICE candidates. Queue candidates that arrive before the corresponding remote description is ready. Reject stale connection-generation messages.
  6. ICE checks candidate pairs and selects a usable path. Observe connection and ICE state; a successful signaling exchange alone is not a successful call.
  7. On hang-up, close channels and connections and stop local media tracks. Remove listeners and expire room membership.

SDP describes negotiated media/session details; it is not the media itself. Trickle ICE sends candidates as they are discovered. In production both sides may negotiate at once. Use the perfect-negotiation pattern to handle offer collisions instead of assuming A is always the only offerer.

Build the smallest useful exercise

Begin with a text data channel, not camera access. Use two peers in one local teaching page to inspect offer/answer and channel events. This loopback experiment avoids a signaling server but does not prove internet connectivity. Follow the MDN data-channel example.

Next, use two browser sessions with an authenticated signaling endpoint and short-lived room membership. Validate message type, size, room, sender and negotiation generation. Keep a bounded pending-candidate queue and a connection deadline. Do not publish raw SDP or candidate addresses in logs.

Use short-lived server-issued TURN credentials. Do not put permanent relay credentials in a public bundle. In an approved network test, force relay-only policy and verify the selected candidate pair rather than guessing from connection success. Test expired credentials and blocked UDP; permitted TURN/TCP or TURN/TLS can provide alternatives but their availability is deployment-specific.

Media and data have different reliability needs

A late audio packet may be less useful than a lost one. Jitter buffers smooth timing variation at a latency cost. Packet loss, bitrate adaptation and congestion affect quality. Reliable application messages may instead need ordered delivery and acknowledgement of the business action.

RTCDataChannel can be configured for ordered or unordered delivery and bounded retransmission behaviour. A successful send does not mean an application effect committed. Check bufferedAmount and pause producers above a bound; resume with bufferedamountlow. Limit message size and split large transfers with an explicit protocol. See data-channel API.

Small peer meshes are easy to describe but each participant may send multiple copies. An SFU forwards selected media streams and changes capacity, trust and bandwidth planning. An MCU mixes media and adds processing. Transport encryption to an SFU does not by itself establish application-level end-to-end secrecy from that SFU. State the actual trust model.

Investigate failures in order

SymptomCheckSafe response
Room joins but no callDescriptions, candidate delivery, generation and ICE stateBound setup time; show a clear failure
Works on LAN, fails remotelySelected candidate pairs, TURN reachability and credentialsTry approved relay path; do not blame codecs first
Call breaks after network switchConnection state and stale candidatesBounded ICE restart with renegotiation; reconnect if necessary
Audio arrives but video stallsTrack state, received frames, loss, bitrate and decoder statisticsReduce video load or offer audio-only mode
Memory grows during transferData-channel bufferedAmount and producer rateApply backpressure and payload limits

A disconnected state may be temporary. Avoid immediate repeated reconnects. Apply a short grace policy, a finite restart budget and a total deadline. Get statistics from getStats(): selected pair, round-trip time, bytes, loss and media-specific counters where available. Compare counter deltas over time, not just cumulative totals. Do not infer one-way delay from RTT without assumptions.

Verify setup latency, relay fraction, reconnect success and quality under packet loss, slow networks and background tabs. Include microphone denial, device removal, caller departure and simultaneous offers. An AI voice assistant also needs interruption handling: stop obsolete playback/generation, keep turn IDs and do not execute tools from partial transcripts.

Interview checks

Can a WebSocket signaling server replace TURN?

No. Signaling exchanges setup messages. TURN relays the negotiated traffic when required. They can run on related infrastructure but serve different protocols and purposes.

Does encrypted transport authenticate a room participant?

No. Bind signaling, room membership and actions to verified application identity. Media permissions and network encryption do not replace that authorization.

MCQ: STUN supplies “address discovery” or “media relay”?

Address discovery. TURN provides relay service.
