Read chart evidence before answering

These six charts are synthetic fixtures. For each, state the value or relationship, its unit, the axis you used and whether the reading is exact. Answer before opening the feedback. Attractive prose earns no evidence points.

1. Linear axis

How much larger is B than A?

Answer and evidence

B is 40 requests/min and A is 20. The difference is 20 requests/min; the ratio is two. Both bars align with labeled ticks. “B increased by 20” would imply time or a causal comparison that the labels do not establish.

2. Logarithmic axis

Do the equal vertical steps represent equal additions?

Answer and evidence

No. Values are 1, 10 and 100. Each step multiplies by ten. Additions are 9 and 90. A point halfway between 10 and 100 on this log axis represents about 31.6, the geometric midpoint, not 55.

3. Dual axes

At the crossing, are requests and latency equal?

Answer and evidence

No. They use different units and different axes. The crossing is a visual coincidence. Read each series against its own scale. This chart also cannot prove that increasing requests caused latency to fall.

4. Truncated axis

Is B twice A?

Answer and evidence

The visible bar height doubles, but the scores are 96 and 98. The absolute increase is two score units. Relative to 96, the increase is about 2.08%. State the nonzero baseline before using bar height to compare magnitude.

5. Missing legend

Did revenue increase?

Answer and evidence

The chart does not identify revenue. You can describe the rising blue solid series and falling brown dashed series, but cannot assign a business meaning. Ask for the legend or source data. Inferring revenue from a familiar color is unsupported.

6. Approximate reading

Can the chart establish 73.142 milliseconds?

Answer and evidence

No. A visual estimate near 70 to 75 ms is reasonable. Exact decimals require a data label or underlying data. SVG coordinates are authoring details, not additional measurement precision available in the displayed chart.

Score support separately

MeasureScoring rule per case
Evidence support, 0 to 22: correct claim and axis/legend evidence; 1: correct claim without the needed evidence; 0: unsupported or wrong claim
Units/precision, 0 to 11: correct units and exact/approximate/unknown distinction; 0: missing or false precision
Readability, 0 to 11: understandable wording; 0: confusing wording

Record all three totals separately: evidence out of 12, units/precision out of 6 and readability out of 6. A fluent wrong answer can score readability one and evidence zero. Preserve fixture number, answer and reviewer notes. No learner or model scores are prefilled.

For a document pipeline, keep the chart's source version, page and crop, including title, units and legend. If a crop removes one of these, expand the evidence or abstain. Ask your teacher to review a dual-axis answer and repeat the exercise tomorrow without opening the feedback.
