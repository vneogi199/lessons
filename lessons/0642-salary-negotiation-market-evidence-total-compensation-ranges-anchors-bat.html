01

Salary negotiation

Salary negotiation is a professional discussion about role scope and the full offer using market evidence, priorities, alternatives, and written terms.

02

market evidence

Market evidence is current role-, level-, skill-, company-, and location-relevant compensation information from credible sources.

03

total compensation

Total compensation combines fixed pay, variable pay, equity under its actual terms, benefits, retirement, leave, relocation support, and other material value.

04

ranges

A compensation range is an interval supported by role and market evidence; ask how the employer maps experience and level within it.

05

anchors

An anchor is an early reference number that can influence later discussion; support it with evidence and avoid false precision.

06

BATNA

BATNA is your best alternative if no agreement is reached. It sets a practical walk-away comparison rather than a threat.

07

priorities

Negotiation priorities rank the terms that matter most so you can trade across salary, level, equity, start date, relocation, flexibility, and development support.

08

scripts

A negotiation script is a short prepared structure for appreciation, evidence, request, question, pause, and response under pressure.

09

counteroffers

A counteroffer proposes specific revised terms with a reason and leaves room for the employer to respond with alternatives.

10

written terms

Written terms are the authoritative offer details to review before acceptance, including conditions, deadlines, equity documents, location, and relocation obligations.

08 · Common mistakes

Memorizing the label

A definition is not enough. Trace an interview decision and its evidence from input to output.

Testing only success

The happy path can hide a weak design. Try this failure: Counter the same offer with base flexible, base fixed, and relocation-risk scenarios without inventing leverage.

Changing too much

Change one condition at a time. Otherwise, you will not know what caused the result.

Guessing
