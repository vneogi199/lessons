04 · Detailed term guide

How they connect: Separate role value and market evidence from personal need, compare base, variable pay, equity, benefits, tax assumptions, relocation, risk, and growth, then exchange justified proposals while protecting alternatives and relationships.

01

Salary negotiation

What it means: Negotiation is a respectful evidence-based discussion of role level, compensation, support, timing, and terms after enough information exists to compare the complete offer.

02

market evidence

What it means: Market evidence is current role-, level-, skill-, company-, and location-relevant compensation information from credible sources.

03

total compensation

What it means: Total compensation combines fixed pay, variable pay, equity under its actual terms, benefits, retirement, leave, relocation support, and other material value.

04

ranges

What it means: A compensation range is an interval supported by role and market evidence; ask how the employer maps experience and level within it.

05

anchors

What it means: An anchor is an early reference number that can influence later discussion; support it with evidence and avoid false precision.

06

BATNA

What it means: BATNA is your best alternative if no agreement is reached. It sets a practical walk-away comparison rather than a threat.

07

priorities

What it means: Negotiation priorities rank the terms that matter most so you can trade across salary, level, equity, start date, relocation, flexibility, and development support.

08

scripts

What it means: A negotiation script is a short prepared structure for appreciation, evidence, request, question, pause, and response under pressure.

09

counteroffers

What it means: A counteroffer proposes specific revised terms with a reason and leaves room for the employer to respond with alternatives.

10

written terms

What it means: Written terms are the authoritative offer details to review before acceptance, including conditions, deadlines, equity documents, location, and relocation obligations.

const comparableAnnual = offer => offer.base + offer.targetBonus + offer.expectedEquity + offer.pension + offer.relocation;
console.table(offers.map(offer => ({ company: offer.company, comparableAnnual: comparableAnnual(offer) })));

  close: "Please send any revision and all conditions in writing so I can compare the complete offer."

08 · Common mistakes

Memorizing the label

A definition is not enough. Trace an interview scenario from input to output.

Testing only success

The happy path can hide a weak design. Try this failure: Counter the same offer with base flexible, base fixed, and relocation-risk scenarios without inventing leverage.

Changing too much

Change one condition at a time. Otherwise, you will not know what caused the result.

Guessing

Check the system instead: Market sources, compensation model, priorities, target, BATNA, scripts, employer response, revised terms, conditions, and written offer.
