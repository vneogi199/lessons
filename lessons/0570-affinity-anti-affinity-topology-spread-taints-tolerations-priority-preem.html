Affinity, anti-affinity, topology spread, taints, tolerations, priority, preemption, disruption budgets, and drains

Kubernetes stores desired state in API objects. Controllers work continuously to make actual state match desired state.

Kubernetes is a control room: you declare desired state through an API, controllers compare it with reality, and node agents keep reconciling until status matches or evidence explains why not.

01

Affinity

Affinity attracts Pods toward nodes or other Pods matching placement criteria.

02

anti-affinity

Anti-affinity separates Pods from matching placements to reduce unwanted colocation.

03

topology spread

Topology spread constraints distribute Pods across failure or location domains under a maximum-skew policy.

04

taints

A taint discourages scheduling, blocks scheduling or evicts Pods according to its effect. A matching toleration permits that boundary but does not guarantee placement. Affinity, resources and topology constraints still apply.

05

tolerations

Tolerations allow Pods to tolerate matching taints; they do not force placement on those nodes.

06

priority

Pod priority influences scheduling and supported preemption behavior, not unlimited access to resources.

07

preemption

Preemption may remove lower-priority Pods to make room for higher-priority work, without guaranteeing immediate successful placement.

08

disruption budgets

PodDisruptionBudgets constrain supported voluntary disruptions; they do not prevent all involuntary failures or guarantee rollout safety.

09

drains

Node drains evict eligible workloads before maintenance while respecting supported disruption and Pod rules.
